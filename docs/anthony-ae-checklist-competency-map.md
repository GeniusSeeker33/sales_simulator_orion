# Anthony AE Checklist — Competency and Evidence Map

**Status:** supporting operational analysis; not a canonical competency framework, rubric, learner assessment, or implementation claim.  
**Authority:** competency definitions and anchors come only from the [Orion sales competencies canonical index](training/orion-sales-competencies.md); progression is governed by the [Orion L1–L5 framework](training/orion-l1-l5.md) and [training and progression record model](training/training-progression-record-model.md).  
**Subject:** the checklist is referred to as the “Anthony AE checklist” for traceability. Its title does not imply that Anthony's records, results, or proficiency are documented here.

## Why Preserve This Analysis

An account-executive checklist can expose useful links among day-to-day work, coaching prompts, evidence opportunities, and production support. Preserving that mapping helps operators ask better questions during Academy and ramp reviews.

It must not become a competing C01–C15 catalog. The canonical competency source retains the stable codes, immutable identifiers, definitions, versions, and B1–B5 behavioral anchors. This document does not reproduce or revise those definitions; exact mappings must be confirmed against the frozen detailed source before operational use.

## What a Checklist Item Can and Cannot Mean

A checklist item can document that a task was assigned, attempted, observed, reviewed, or completed. Depending on context, it may provide an **opportunity** to observe one or more competencies.

It does not by itself prove proficiency. In particular:

- a checked box is not competency evidence unless an attributable observation satisfies the canonical evidence rules;
- task completion is not a human-reviewed competency conclusion;
- checklist completion percentage is not an overall Behavioral Level B1–B5;
- checklist completion is not a production performance level L1–L5;
- a manager's or AI system's informal label is not an approved decision; and
- repeated references to the same call, order, or observation do not create independent evidence.

## Operational Mapping Method

For each checklist item, an operator should record or propose the following without changing the canonical framework:

| Mapping field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Checklist item and version | Preserves the exact operational expectation that applied. |
| Workflow phase | Locates the item in Academy, supported ramp, or production operations. |
| Task or knowledge intent | States what the item asks the AE to do or understand. |
| Candidate competency code(s) | Provides a hypothesis for later confirmation against canonical C01–C15 definitions; it is not a new definition. |
| Observable behavior | Describes what a reviewer could actually see or hear, avoiding personality inference. |
| Primary source occurrence | Identifies the simulation, coaching observation, or real-work occurrence only once. |
| Context and opportunity | Captures account/territory context, difficulty, support, and whether a fair opportunity existed. |
| Evidence status | Distinguishes proposed, human-verified, rejected, disputed, unavailable, stale, or technically failed material. |
| Reviewer and framework versions | Makes the conclusion attributable and reproducible. |
| Follow-up | Captures practice, coaching, reassessment, or an evidence gap without treating it as failure. |

Candidate mappings should be reviewed by the canonical framework owner. If the checklist uses words that resemble a competency but mean something different, the checklist wording should be clarified rather than changing the canonical competency.

## Practical Coverage Map

The following preserves the useful categories of the AE analysis while avoiding unverified claims about exact C01–C15 codes. Canonical codes should be attached only after checking the detailed, versioned competency source.

| AE operational area | Example checklist evidence opportunity | What a reviewer may examine | Important limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preparation and account context | prepare an account plan, stakeholders, desired outcome, and call hypothesis | relevance of preparation, use of available context, and adaptation when assumptions change | preparation artifacts show process, not necessarily live execution or commercial impact |
| Opening and agenda | open a meeting, establish purpose, and confirm time/agenda | clarity, mutual agreement, professionalism, and responsiveness | attendance or a completed template is not evidence of behavioral quality |
| Discovery | ask, sequence, and follow up on questions; confirm understanding | listening, curiosity, business relevance, and accuracy of synthesis | question count rewards volume and must not replace review of behavior and context |
| Value alignment | connect a proposed solution to verified needs and constraints | reasoning, relevance, accuracy, and avoidance of unsupported claims | a proposal sent or product mentioned does not show that value was understood |
| Objection handling | clarify the concern, respond, and check resolution | composure, diagnosis, accuracy, and collaborative response | an objection's disappearance may reflect customer choice, not AE proficiency |
| Commercial and process alignment | clarify stakeholders, decision process, timing, and appropriate next steps | transparent expectations, mutual commitments, and accurate documentation | forecast movement or stage changes can be administrative and are not proficiency by themselves |
| Product/order accuracy | follow approved product, pricing, order, and handoff procedures | accuracy, policy adherence, escalation, and correction behavior | an error-free order may offer little evidence about other competencies; a system failure is not a behavioral deficit |
| Relationship and account stewardship | conduct follow-up, coordinate resolution, and maintain agreed communication | reliability, ownership, boundaries, collaboration, and learning across interactions | retention or account growth is affected by product, service, pricing, territory, and market conditions |
| Coaching and improvement | prepare for coaching, reflect on evidence, practice, and follow through | quality of reflection, use of feedback, and behavior change across distinct observations | acknowledgment means receipt, not agreement; practice quantity is not proficiency |
| Ethical and compliant conduct | use approved claims, protect information, and escalate risks | observable policy-aligned decisions and appropriate escalation | checklist completion cannot waive compliance review or prove conduct outside the observed context |

This table organizes observation opportunities, not behavioral anchors. Reviewers must consult the applicable canonical competency version before linking an occurrence to a competency or criterion.

## Evidence Packet Example

For a checklist item such as “confirm the customer's decision process,” a useful packet could include:

1. the checklist and item version;
2. the attributable call or simulation occurrence and its approved evidence reference;
3. role, scenario/account context, opportunity, support, and occurrence time;
4. the exact competency and anchor versions proposed by the reviewer;
5. the observable behavior and relevant excerpt or structured note;
6. verification, admissibility, freshness, limitations, and any contrary evidence; and
7. a human review finding and rationale, kept separate from the source observation.

If the call was unavailable, the customer did not provide an opportunity, or a recording failed, record that state. Do not convert it to “not demonstrated,” zero, or a low band without the governed human review and adequate evidence required by the canonical model.

## Outcomes and Activity Measures

The checklist may reference calls, meetings, opportunities, orders, sales volume, revenue, account growth, retention, or other outcomes because they matter operationally. Those facts belong to Performance Intelligence and reporting, with the correct source-of-truth and context. They do **not** automatically determine:

- whether a checklist behavior occurred well;
- proficiency for any individual competency;
- an overall Behavioral Level B1–B5; or
- an approved production performance level L1–L5.

For example, strong revenue can coexist with weak or unavailable behavioral evidence, and low order volume can coexist with strong observed behaviors in a constrained territory. Any reviewer use of outcomes must be allowed by the pinned framework, attributable, contextualized, and explained. Financial data remains under the authority described in the [reporting and integration plan](reporting/orion-reporting-and-integration-plan.md).

## Relationship to Academy, B1–B5, and L1–L5

The [Sales Academy transition analysis](orion-sales-academy-production-level-transition.md) describes four separate views:

1. Academy/ramp workflow status;
2. production performance level L1–L5;
3. individual competency proficiency and its evidence; and
4. an overall Behavioral Level B1–B5, when a governed holistic review exists.

The Anthony AE checklist can support Academy assignments, coaching conversations, and evidence discovery across those views. It cannot collapse them. There is no checklist-to-B formula, competency-average-to-B formula, B-to-L conversion, or outcome-to-level rule.

## Operational Use Guardrails

Before using the checklist in a workflow:

- version and assign an accountable owner to the checklist;
- verify candidate competency mappings against the detailed canonical C01–C15 source;
- define what counts as completion separately from what counts as admissible evidence;
- train reviewers to record observed behavior rather than inferred traits;
- preserve source provenance, reviewer identity, framework version, and employment/role scope;
- distinguish missing opportunity, technical failure, dispute, and evidence insufficiency from a demonstrated gap;
- avoid collecting recordings, transcripts, or customer data unless approved, necessary, access-controlled, and retained appropriately;
- give the learner visibility into consequential human decisions and an appropriate response path; and
- audit reports and dashboards for implied automatic scoring or conversion.

Until those controls exist, this map should be used as design and coaching analysis only.
